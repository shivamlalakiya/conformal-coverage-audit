Why statsforecast and not sktime. An earlier version of this probe ran
sktime's public predict_interval and its measured coverage sat four
standard errors ABOVE the prediction. The cause is in this deposit
already: that path reads the offset-1 diagonal of residuals_matrix_,
which holds TWO-step residuals, so its calibration scores are not
misaligned with the one-step test residual for any dataset.
Rebuilding arm B on the aligned diagonal does not repair the comparison
either -- it makes the two arms different constructions, and the
containment assertion in run_real_data says so at the first fit. So the
attributable claim runs on a helper with no diagonal in its path:
statsforecast's scores are captured from its own conformal call.

WHAT THIS SETTLES, AND WHAT AN EARLIER VERSION OF IT GOT WRONG.
The bracket above is the test, and it passes everywhere the threshold is an
order statistic. An earlier run of this probe used r/(n+1) as a POINT
prediction and reported the n=20 cells as three and a half standard errors
below it. They were not below anything: r is the least admissible order
statistic for the returned cutoff, so r/(n+1) is Proposition 2's UPPER bound and
(r-1)/(n+1) its lower one. The interval has width 1/21 at n=20 and 1/51 at
n=50, which is exactly why the mistake looked like a small-n anomaly: the
error was in the prediction and it scaled with the gap width.

So the attributable statement is this. On series carrying a real fitted
dynamic and a real innovation law, with the one property that breaks
attribution removed, a shipped helper's ABSOLUTE coverage falls where the
index it landed on says it must -- and the shortfall against nominal is
therefore the index convention and not the archive. That is the sentence
the paired arms cannot produce, and it needs no pairing to state.

Agreement between the two generators is what removes the fitted AR(1) from
the argument. The walk rows need no specification at all: a last-value
forecaster leaves errors on a random walk that ARE the innovations, so
nothing but the resampling stands between the real series and the claim.
