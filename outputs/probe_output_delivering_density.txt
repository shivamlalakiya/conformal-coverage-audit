WHAT THE FORM EXPLAINS THAT THE MEASURED TABLE COULD NOT

(a) The factor of two. Below alpha = 1/2, `higher` lands honestly at twice
    the sizes `inverted_cdf` does, because its condition takes in a stretch
    of 2(d-p) residues against the other's (d-p).

(b) Why inverted_cdf and weibull looked alike. Where alpha = 1/d the two
    expressions alpha and 1/d evaluate to one number, which is why the pair
    agrees at 9/10, 19/20 and 99/100. The LEVEL causes that, not anything
    about either definition. Away from unit fractions they come apart:
      L = 5/7     inverted_cdf 2/7     vs weibull 1/7     ratio 2.000
      L = 11/13   inverted_cdf 2/13    vs weibull 1/13    ratio 2.000
      L = 17/21   inverted_cdf 4/21    vs weibull 1/21    ratio 4.000

    Hence the standing rule that a non-unit-fraction level appears in every
    sweep. It is not caution: without one, two of these definitions are
    indistinguishable, and the closed form shows why.

(c) A number in place of an adjective. For the rounding definitions the
    proportion of honest calibration sizes is alpha itself, so asking for a
    TIGHTER level leaves FEWER sizes that can honour it -- one in a hundred
    at 0.99. Precisely the wrong way round for anyone who tightens a level
    expecting a safer answer.
