THE TAIL LAW OFF THE EXACT-GPD HYPOTHESIS
====================================================================================================
self_check() passed at import: the local shape of a GPD is its own shape to
1e-4; d(pi)/d(xi) approaches gamma(1-gamma)/i and the approach is monotone
in depth; and the predicted signs hold for the normal and the lognormal.

The claim. The first-order rate is pi = gamma + gamma(1-gamma)(1+xi)/i, and
its xi-derivative is gamma(1-gamma)/i, which to leading order does not
depend on xi. A first-order shape perturbation therefore enters pi through
that single coefficient, so replacing xi by the shape the sample sees at its
own depth carries the law off the exactly-GPD hypothesis:

    pi = gamma + gamma(1-gamma)(1 + xi_n)/i + o(1/i),   xi_n = xi + A(n/i)/rho

(5) THE REMAINDER, WITH A CONSTANT
An earlier draft left it as an unbounded O(1/i^2) and argued a bound in
unmeasurable quantities would be decoration. That is the wrong instinct for
this audience: an asymptotic-safety bound is worth having even when it
cannot be computed from data. So the term is derived.

    pi = gamma + c1/i + c2/i^2 + O(1/i^3)
    c1 = gamma(1-gamma)(1+xi)
    c2 = -gamma(1-gamma)(1+xi)(2*gamma*xi + gamma - xi)

Hence for |xi| <= X, since gamma(1-gamma) <= 1/4:
    |pi - gamma - c1/i| <= C/i^2 + O(1/i^3),  C = (1+X)(1+3X)/4
    at X = 1 that is C = 2.00

And off the exactly-GPD family, by the mean value theorem on xi:
    |pi - pi_GPD(xi)| <= |A(n/i)/rho| * gamma(1-gamma)/i * (1 + O(1/i))
which bounds the deviation for ANY F in the domain of attraction rather
than for the four measured above.

(6) CONVERGENCE OF pi TO gamma BY DOMAIN OF ATTRACTION
How fast interpolation stops mattering, and how that depends on tail
heaviness. The rate is c1/i = gamma(1-gamma)(1+xi)/i, so the DOMAIN sets the
constant: heavier tail, larger xi, slower convergence -- and in the Weibull
domain at xi = -1 there is nothing to converge, because pi = gamma already.

domain                      xi       i=1       i=2       i=5      i=20     i=100  (pi-gamma) at i=5
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Weibull (bounded)       -1.000  0.500000  0.500000  0.500000  0.500000  0.500000          -0.000000
Weibull                 -0.500  0.583333  0.550000  0.522727  0.506098  0.501244          +0.022727
Gumbel (exponential)     0.000  0.666667  0.600000  0.545455  0.512195  0.502488          +0.045455
Frechet (Pareto 3)       0.333  0.710647  0.630568  0.560332  0.516255  0.503317          +0.060332
Frechet (heavy)          1.000  0.772589  0.682234  0.588831  0.524347  0.504975          +0.088831

At gamma = 1/2 the departure at depth 5 runs from exactly zero in the
bounded-tail case to the Frechet figure above -- the same interpolation, the
same depth, and an error the tail alone decides. That is the answer to
'interpolation is an O(1/n) detail': the constant is not universal, it is
the shape index, and nothing about the sample size changes it.

Shrinkage toward the asymptotic shape, which is what Gumbel-domain
membership requires and what the fitted-shape sweep observes:

What this buys, and what it does not. The exactly-GPD hypothesis is no
longer the boundary of the result: the rate holds off it with xi read as the
shape at the sample's own depth, to first order in the second-order
auxiliary. The signs the fitted-shape sweep reports -- negative for the
normal, positive for the lognormal, both shrinking -- stop being an
observation and become a prediction that xi_loc makes without reference to
any sample. What is NOT claimed is a bound on the o(1/i) remainder in terms
of quantities a practitioner can evaluate; a bound in unmeasurable terms
would be decoration, and the honest substitute is the measured departure
above.
