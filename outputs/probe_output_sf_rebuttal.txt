VERDICT, corrected -- an earlier draft of this script asserted the
wider-always property was exact by monotonicity.  Running it disproves
that.  The upper rail ceil((n+1)q)/n >= q always, so it never narrows.
The LOWER rail floor((n+1)q)/n is NOT bounded above by q:
    n=  39  q=0.0250  floor((n+1)q)/n = 0.025641  > q   -> lower bound moves IN
    n=  79  q=0.0250  floor((n+1)q)/n = 0.025316  > q   -> lower bound moves IN
    n= 119  q=0.0250  floor((n+1)q)/n = 0.025210  > q   -> lower bound moves IN
So: inside the clamp window the clamped interval is strictly wider than
plain (claim 3 holds where it was asserted).  Outside it, at the cells
where (n+1)q lands just above an integer, the floor rule is mildly
ANTI-conservative on the lower rail relative to plain.  Both statements
are needed; neither alone describes _oriented_index.

SEPARATE OBSERVATION (unfiled, needs its own adjudication):
