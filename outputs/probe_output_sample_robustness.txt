share their history, so each series contributes one mean paired difference
and the s.e. is taken across series. se_naive treats every origin as an
independent draw; it is printed so the dependence is visible rather than
assumed. se exceeds se_naive where origins within a series are positively
correlated and falls below it where they are not, so se_ratio goes BOTH ways
and this output used to claim the two coincide off the positive side. They do
not: read se_ratio per cell. The sharp narrowing sits in the cells where arm
B is vacuous at every point, where the paired difference is 1 minus a
coverage indicator rather than a comparison of two index rules.

req_med=0 marks a cell with no feasible required rank at all -- read it with
the infeasible count on the same line, never as a rank of zero.

unit=rank when absolute-score bands are symmetric; unit=span for a helper
resolving a pair of levels on signed residuals, so arm B uses the span
of gaps that the required coverage asks for. An asymmetric half-width
does not pick any order statistic, so those two figures stay apart.
gains/losses/changed are counted over the test points. `changed` is NOT
delta x points: a clustered mean of per-series means does not equal that, and
gains - losses is the net rather than the count. losses must read 0 wherever
arm B contains arm A, which each arm asserts per fit.

A positive delta means the required rank covers more than the shipped call.
Raw series lack exchangeability, so an absolute coverage figure is still not
attributable to the convention -- the paired delta is what carries the claim.
What this probe removes is the selection and resolution objections to that
delta, not the exchangeability caveat, which no amount of data can remove.
